    The artist says, describing the illustration on this T-shirt:
    “Esus is an ancient Gallic deity, known through a few Roman testimonies in which he appears associated with a tree and with an enigmatic ritual context. His original nature remains unknown. Before the encounter with the classical world, we have no anthropomorphic depictions of how the Gallic peoples might have conceived the sacred: forces, entities and manifestations of nature, perhaps not sharply distinguished from it.
    In many cultures, from shamanic traditions to Tantric representations and the symbolic evidence of prehistory, the sacred is also expressed through signs, geometries and abstract forms, capable of evoking invisible presences and forces without depicting them directly. A tree might not have been the symbol or dwelling of a deity, but its very manifestation. It is an organism that passes through cycles of apparent death and renewal: it loses its leaves, seems to halt its activity, and then sprouts again. In many traditions it is a manifestation of the continuity of life and of regeneration. The literary sources and the commentaries on Lucan associate Esus with sacrificial rituals. This allows us to explore the theme of passage through death, of transformation conceived as a return but in different forms, of the dissolution of the individual and their subsequent rebirth through other manifestations of nature.
    I am always looking for ways to represent gods and spirits we have forgotten. In some cases I recognize them in popular saints, at other times in nature (and this will certainly be part of my future work), but this piece stems from a free reflection on these possibilities, imagining Esus not through a figurative image but through a language of forms and signs. In this way I avoid the anthropomorphization we have grown accustomed to since the classical period, which greatly restricts our ability to imagine and understand certain things.”
